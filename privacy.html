Lectern Notes Privacy Policy

Effective 30 September 2026 · Applies to Lectern Notes for iPad, version 1.0 and later

Lectern Notes records your lectures, transcribes them on your iPad, and helps you turn the transcript into study material. It is made by Max McGee, an individual developer (“I”, “me”). This page explains exactly what the app does with your information. The short version is below; the details follow.

  • I do not collect your data. The app has no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server of mine that anything is sent to.
  • Your recordings, transcripts and study guides stay on your iPad, unless you choose to share or export them.
  • Transcription happens on your iPad. Audio is not sent anywhere unless you set up your own Lectern server (an advanced option that is off by default).
  • If you use Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini to write a study guide, the app asks your permission first, then copies a prompt containing your transcript for you to paste into that service. The app itself sends nothing to those companies.
  • The first time you open the app it downloads a speech-recognition model (about 490 MB) from Hugging Face, after you tap Download. No personal information is sent with that download. It uses whatever connection your iPad has, including mobile data.

1. Information stored on your iPad

The app keeps the following inside its own storage on your iPad. I cannot see any of it.

  • Audio recordings of lectures you record or import.
  • Transcripts made from that audio.
  • Study material: notes, summaries, quizzes, flashcards and worked problems, whether written on your iPad or pasted back from a chat service.
  • Files you add, such as lecture slides (PDF or PowerPoint), videos and recordings you open in the app, and the text read from them.
  • Courses you create: their names, codes and vocabulary lists.
  • Settings, such as which speech model and chat service you prefer, and which chat services you have allowed.
  • Technical logs: how long each piece of audio took to transcribe, when a recording started and stopped, and similar timing and status records. These logs contain no audio and none of the words that were spoken.
  • Replies the app could not read: when a chat service’s reply you paste cannot be imported, its text is kept (the most recent 20) so the problem can be looked into. These stay on your iPad and are never included in a diagnostics report.

When you open a recording, video or document from another app (for example Voice Memos or Files), the app reads the file’s creation date so the lecture is dated when the class happened.

This information is included in your iPad’s backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) according to your own backup settings, and is handled by Apple under Apple’s terms. The downloaded speech model is excluded from backups. Deleting a lecture in the app deletes its recording, transcript and study material. Deleting the app deletes everything it stored.

2. The microphone

The app uses the microphone only after you press Record, and only until you press Stop. So that a lecture is not cut short, recording continues if you lock the screen or switch to another app; iPadOS shows its microphone indicator for as long as the app is recording. The app does not listen at any other time.

3. Processing on your iPad

  • Transcription uses the open-source Whisper speech model, run on your iPad through WhisperKit. Your audio is not uploaded to do this.
  • Live preview text shown while you record comes from Apple’s on-device speech recogniser. iPadOS may download Apple’s English speech assets to your iPad to make this possible; that download is managed by Apple.
  • Apple Intelligence. On iPads where Apple Intelligence is available and turned on, the app can use Apple’s on-device language model to write notes and attempt worked problems. This runs on your iPad through Apple’s Foundation Models framework. The app does not send your transcript to Apple to do this. These drafts are labelled in the app as a fast first draft that can contain mistakes.

5. Chat services: Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini

You can ask a chat service you already use to write a study guide or solve a worked problem. Lectern Notes does this without an API key and without an account of its own:

  1. The first time you choose a service, the app shows what will be shared and with which company, and asks for your permission. If you cancel, nothing is copied or opened.
  2. With your permission, the app copies a prompt to your clipboard and opens the service’s website or app. You paste the prompt there. The app does not send it.
  3. You copy the reply and tap Paste in Lectern Notes. The reply is checked and stored on your iPad.

Depending on what you asked for, the prompt you paste can contain:

  • the transcript of the lecture, or the part of it around one worked problem;
  • the lecture’s title and date, and the course’s name, code and vocabulary list;
  • text read from slides you added to the lecture;
  • the times at which worked examples were detected, and, for a single problem, the app’s previous attempt at it and the checker’s notes on it;
  • instructions telling the chat service how to format its reply.

The prompt never contains your audio. Once you paste it, the company that runs the service receives it and handles it under its own terms and privacy policy, and under any settings in your account with them (for example whether your chats may be used to train their models):

  • Claude is run by Anthropic: Anthropic Privacy Policy
  • ChatGPT is run by OpenAI: OpenAI Privacy Policy
  • Gemini is run by Google: Google Privacy Policy

You can withdraw the permission you gave for any service at any time in Settings ▸ About ▸ Privacy; the app will ask again before it next opens that service. Withdrawing permission does not delete anything you already pasted into a chat service; do that in your account with that company.

To paste a reply, the app uses the system Paste button, so it reads your clipboard only when you tap it.

6. Your own Lectern server (advanced, off by default)

Some people run the Lectern transcription server on another computer of their own and reach it over a private network such as Tailscale. This is an advanced option in Settings ▸ About ▸ Advanced. If, and only if, you turn it on and enter an address, the app sends that server:

  • pieces of audio while you record, so the server can transcribe them, together with the previous sentence of the transcript and the course vocabulary to help recognition;
  • if the server offers a live study guide: the lecture’s title, the course’s name, its vocabulary list and the kind of guide wanted, when a recording starts;
  • if the server offers worked-problem solutions: the transcript text around each worked problem, its label, the course vocabulary and, when the app asks for a correction, the checker’s notes;
  • if you choose Re-transcribe on server for a finished lecture: the whole lecture’s audio, with its title, course name, date and vocabulary. The app asks the server to delete that audio once the transcript is back.

Each lecture sent to the server is identified by a random number the app made for it, which says nothing about you.

The server is yours; I do not operate one and receive none of this. The address is stored in the app’s settings and the access token in the iPad’s Keychain. Recording always saves audio on your iPad first, whether or not a server is used.

7. Sharing and exporting

Nothing leaves the app unless you choose to send it:

  • Export saves a study guide or transcript as Markdown or PDF to a place you pick, or hands it to the iPadOS share sheet.
  • Send diagnostics (Settings ▸ Storage) builds a small report, shows you what it contains, and hands it to the share sheet only when you tap Share. You choose who receives it. It contains: the iPad model, iPadOS version, app version and build, memory size, free storage, thermal state, battery level, whether Low Power Mode is on, the selected speech model, whether Apple Intelligence is available, the app’s own status log lines, and the timing logs described in section 1. It contains no audio, transcript or study-guide text. If you send it to me, I use it only to investigate the problem you reported, and delete it when I no longer need it for that.

8. Information Apple may share with me

If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPad’s settings (Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Analytics & Improvements), Apple may share crash reports and aggregated usage statistics about Lectern Notes with me. That is controlled by you and handled under Apple’s Privacy Policy. If you test a beta through TestFlight, feedback and crash reports you choose to send reach me through Apple.

9. Recording other people

Lectures include other people’s voices. Many universities and colleges have rules about recording classes, and the law on recording differs between places. Please check your institution’s policy and get permission where it is required. What you record, and what you share, is your responsibility.

10. Children

Lectern Notes is intended for students in further and higher education and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone.

11. Your choices and rights

Because I hold no personal information about you, there is nothing for me to access, correct, export or delete on your behalf. Everything the app stores is under your control on your iPad: delete a lecture in the app, or delete the app to remove all of it. If you sent me a diagnostics report or an email, you can ask me to delete it using the contact below.

12. Changes to this policy

If a future version changes what the app does with your information, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date above will change. The version shown in the app is the one that applies to the version you have installed.
